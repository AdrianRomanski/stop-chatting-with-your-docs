That was the theory. Here's how it runs for me, with a real book.

(steps) Then the book moves through five steps in kb-template: read, notes, then progressive summarization on the chapters: bold, highlight, a summary in my own words. You saw those layers on the Distill slide.

(synthesize, diamond) Bold, highlight, summary: that's distillation. The last step is different: I synthesize one idea into a concept note. That's synthesis, and it stays mine. It's the only thing the coach spars. Not the book, not my raw notes: the idea, in my words.

<!--
⏱ 1:05 · script: [STAGE ConceptNote "the last layer"] One idea, in *your* words

My real page. Building a Second Brain, chapter 7, the way I marked it. (1 raw) Layer one: the page as it is.

(2 bold) Layer two: I bold what's interesting. Here, the whole technique, four lines.

(3 highlight, one phrase at a time) Layer three: inside the bold, I highlight. Main points of a note. Distilling the essence. Layers.

(4 summary on top, the unmarked paragraph folds away) Layer four: a summary in my own words. "When I find something interesting I bold it, then I highlight parts of the bold, and at the end I add a summary."

That was distillation. Now synthesis: the concept note. (the card with the diamond builds) One idea, in my words. First field, the core assertions. (arrow from my summary) Drafted from my summary and my answers, never from the book, because whoever writes them decides what passing means. (one after another) The learner identifies interesting text by bolding it. Then highlights portions of the bolded text. Then creates a fourth layer, a summary. Three of my six. (pill) And they're what the judge grades me against, later.

Then the forbidden buzzwords. (chips, crossed one by one) Gemma suggests the words I'd hide behind: distill, the concept's own name. Main points, the author's phrase. Important parts, which says nothing. Each one names the idea instead of explaining it. Use one in an answer and the coach sends it back: say it again as the mechanism, in plain words.

And the source passages: the author's words, quoted from the passage I marked. (yellow arrow) The vault checks them word for word against the page, (green check, page 100) every time it reads the note. The coach quotes them back when I'm stuck on a gap.
-->
